# sn46.14: SN 46.14 Paṭhamagilānasutta — Sick (1st)

Coverage status we recorded: **partly encoded**

What we say we encoded: The Buddha to the sick Mahākassapa: the seven awakening factors, rightly explained, developed and cultivated, lead to direct knowledge, awakening and extinguishment (3.1-3.4).

What we say we left out: The setting and the exchange about his pain (1.1-2.2). Mahākassapa's affirmation (3.5-3.6). The narrative that he recovered from the illness (4.1-4.4): narrated, not taught; the text does not say the factors cure illness.

sn46.14:0.3 | Sick (1st) | Paṭhamagilānasutta
sn46.14:1.1 | At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels’ feeding ground. | Ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe.
sn46.14:1.2 | Now at that time Venerable Mahākassapa was staying in the Pipphali cave, and he was sick, suffering, gravely ill. | Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā mahākassapo pippaliguhāyaṁ viharati ābādhiko dukkhito bāḷhagilāno.
sn46.14:1.3 | Then in the late afternoon, the Buddha came out of retreat, went to Venerable Mahākassapa, sat down on the seat spread out, and said to him: | Atha kho bhagavā sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yenāyasmā mahākassapo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Nisajja kho bhagavā āyasmantaṁ mahākassapaṁ etadavoca:
sn46.14:2.1 | “I hope you’re keeping well, Kassapa, I hope you’re all right. I hope your pain is fading, not growing; that its fading is evident, not its growing.” | “Kacci te, kassapa, khamanīyaṁ kacci yāpanīyaṁ? Kacci dukkhā vedanā paṭikkamanti, no abhikkamanti; paṭikkamosānaṁ paññāyati, no abhikkamo”ti?
sn46.14:2.2 | “Sir, I’m not keeping well, I’m not getting by. My pain is terrible and growing, not fading; its growing is evident, not its fading.” | “Na me, bhante, khamanīyaṁ, na yāpanīyaṁ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṁ paññāyati, no paṭikkamo”ti.
sn46.14:3.1 | “Kassapa, I’ve rightly explained these seven awakening factors. When developed and cultivated, they lead to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to extinguishment. | “Sattime, kassapa, bojjhaṅgā mayā sammadakkhātā bhāvitā bahulīkatā abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattanti.
sn46.14:3.2 | What seven? | Katame satta?
sn46.14:3.3 | The awakening factors of mindfulness, investigation of principles, energy, rapture, tranquility, immersion, and equanimity. | Satisambojjhaṅgo kho, kassapa, mayā sammadakkhāto bhāvito bahulīkato abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati …pe… upekkhāsambojjhaṅgo kho, kassapa, mayā sammadakkhāto bhāvito bahulīkato abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati.
sn46.14:3.4 | These are the seven awakening factors that I’ve rightly explained. When developed and cultivated, they lead to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to extinguishment.” | Ime kho, kassapa, satta bojjhaṅgā mayā sammadakkhātā bhāvitā bahulīkatā abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattantī”ti.
sn46.14:3.5 | “Indeed, Blessed One, these are awakening factors! | “Taggha, bhagavā, bojjhaṅgā;
sn46.14:3.6 | Indeed, Holy One, these are awakening factors!” | taggha, sugata, bojjhaṅgā”ti.
sn46.14:4.1 | That is what the Buddha said. | Idamavoca bhagavā.
sn46.14:4.2 | Satisfied, Venerable Mahākassapa approved what the Buddha said. | Attamano āyasmā mahākassapo bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandi.
sn46.14:4.3 | And that’s how he recovered from that illness. | Vuṭṭhahi cāyasmā mahākassapo tamhā ābādhā.
sn46.14:4.4 |  | Tathāpahīno cāyasmato mahākassapassa so ābādho ahosīti.
