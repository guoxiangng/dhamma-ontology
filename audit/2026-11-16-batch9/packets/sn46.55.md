# sn46.55: SN 46.55 Saṅgāravasutta — With Saṅgārava

Coverage status we recorded: **partly encoded**

What we say we encoded: When the heart is overcome by each of five (sensual lust, ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and remorse, doubt) and the escape from it isn't understood, one doesn't truly know or see what is good for oneself, for another or for both (3.1, 5.1, 7.1, 9.1, 11.1); the five similes of a bowl of water that is dyed, boiling, covered with moss, stirred by the wind, or muddy in the dark (4, 6, 8, 10, 12; D22); developing the awakening factors leads to the realization of the fruit of knowledge and freedom (23.1, 23.3-23.4).

What we say we left out: The reverse teaching, when the heart is not overcome and the escape is understood (13.1-22.4), and its five similes of clear water: the ontology has no concept for the absence of a quality, so the reverse similes stay out too (D22). The consequence the brahmin asked about, long-rehearsed hymns not springing to mind (2.1-2.2, 12.4, 22.4): kept in the claims' notes and the similes' points, not modelled as a claim. That the awakening factors are not obstacles, hindrances or corruptions of the mind (23.1): no pattern yet. The setting and Saṅgārava's going for refuge (1.1-1.3, 24.1-24.3).
